Choosing a game for your child

This is the corner of Wisely Arcade built for children aged 5 to 9 — and for the parents choosing for them. Every game here is 100% free and family-safe, simple enough for small hands, and every one carries a small life lesson: patience, kindness, finishing what you start. There are no downloads and no accounts — you just open a game and play. And because every game has 100 levels that get a little tougher as you climb, a game your child loves today will still challenge them next month. You stay. You climb. You win.

How to pick

  • A first game ever? Start with Animal Memory Match — no timer, no pressure, just flipping cards and finding the animals.
  • A child who can't sit still? Forest Dash — running, jumping and sliding through a magical forest, in short, lively bursts.
  • A careful, thinking child? Bubble Pop — aim the shot, match the colours, plan before you pop.
  • Family time together? Ludo — the classic board race, pass-and-play on one screen, so parent and child play as equals.
  • A little artist? Paint the Rocket — tap-to-colour a rocket, then watch it blast off. There are no wrong colours.
  • Learning to read? Missing Letters — a letter has run away from the word; the picture gives the clue.
  • First numbers? Math Quest — counting apples at the start, real sums as they climb.
  • A puzzle thinker? IQ Quest — patterns and odd-one-out, gentle at first, genuinely clever later.
  • A child who asks "why?" all day? GK Quest — animals, space, science, history and sport, and every answer teaches a true fact.
  • A child who gives up quickly? Any game here. The levels climb gently, one small win at a time, and every lesson is about keeping going.

Honest comparisons

  • Animal Memory Match vs Forest Dash: one is calm and seated — memory and attention. The other is quick feet and quick reactions. Pick by your child's mood, not their age.
  • Bubble Pop vs Ludo: Bubble Pop is a solo aim-and-plan puzzle. Ludo is the one to choose when siblings or parents want to play together.
  • Forest Dash vs Bubble Pop: both reward quick eyes, but Forest Dash tests reflexes while Bubble Pop tests aim — dashers run, poppers think first.
  • Missing Letters vs Math Quest: the same gentle climb in two school skills — letters and words in one, numbers and sums in the other. Most children end up playing both.

How to Play

  1. Read the message on the card carefully — who sent it, what it asks, and how it makes you feel.
  2. Tap 🚩 Scam or ✅ Looks safe.
  3. After every answer, read why — the red flag you missed, or the safety sign you spotted — and the golden rule it teaches.
  4. Each level has 5 messages. Get 4 or more right to pass and unlock the next level.
  5. There are 100 levels. Early scams are loud and obvious; by level 61 they whisper — fake sender names, one wrong letter, pressure dressed as politeness.

Controls

  • Mouse / touch: tap or click one of the two big buttons.
  • Wrong answer? That is the best way to learn — the explanation shows you exactly what to look for next time.

The LessonIf it rushes you, flatters you, or asks for secrets — stop

Almost every scam ever made uses the same three tricks. It rushes you, so you act before you think. It flatters or frightens you, so feelings drive instead of facts. And it asks for secrets — a password, an OTP, card details — because secrets are the keys to your money and your accounts.

Your protection is a pause. Real banks, real schools and real companies can wait ten minutes while you check. Scammers cannot — their whole trick dies the moment you slow down, read the address letter by letter, and ask, "Why is this message in such a hurry?" Learn to spot that hurry here, where mistakes cost nothing, and you will spot it out there, where they do.

About This Game

Spot the Scam trains critical thinking and digital safety — reading sender addresses, questioning urgency, and knowing which requests are never legitimate. It is made for ages 10 and up: older kids, teens, and adults who want sharper scam radar. Every message in the game is fictional, and every answer teaches a real rule used by banks and safety experts.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you play Spot the Scam?

Read the message on the card, then tap "Scam" or "Looks safe". After each answer you see why — the red flag or the safety sign — plus a golden rule to remember.

How many levels are there?

There are 100 levels, with 5 messages in each level. Get 4 or more right to pass. Messages start obvious and become much sneakier as you climb.

Is Spot the Scam based on real scams?

Every message is fictional, but each one copies a real trick scammers use — fake prize wins, OTP theft, look-alike addresses, hijacked friend accounts and boss fraud — so the lessons work in real life.

Who is Spot the Scam for?

It is made for ages 10 and up — anyone who uses a phone, email or social media. Parents often play it with kids as a safety lesson.

What does Spot the Scam teach?

It teaches that if a message rushes you, flatters you, or asks for secrets, you should stop and check. Real organisations never ask for PINs, passwords or OTP codes.

How to Play

  1. Look at the item and read its story — the story is the clue.
  2. Tap 💙 Need it if it keeps you safe, healthy, fed, or learning. Tap 💜 Want it if it is fun, tasty, pretty, or extra.
  3. Every want you spot correctly drops its price in coins into your savings jar — money you did not spend is money saved.
  4. Each level has 8 items — 4 needs and 4 wants. Sort 6 or more correctly to pass.
  5. There are 100 levels. Watch out: the same item can flip! Broken shoes are a need; shiny extra shoes are a want.

Controls

  • Mouse / touch: tap or click one of the two big buttons.
  • Wrong answer? Read the story again in the explanation — the clue was hiding there.

The LessonNeeds first, then saving, then wants

A need keeps you alive, healthy, safe, and learning: food, water, warm clothes, medicine, school things. A want is everything else — fun, tasty, shiny, extra. Wants are not bad! A life with no treats would be a dull life. But money has an order: needs first, a little saving second, wants last — with whatever is left.

The secret this game teaches is that the same thing can be both. Shoes are a need when yours have holes, and a want when yours are fine and you just like the colour. Smart spenders do not ask "do I like it?" — they ask "what is its job in my life right now?" Ask that question here, and one day your grown-up money will thank you.

About This Game

Needs vs Wants teaches money sense and decision-making — telling essentials from extras, noticing how stories and adverts push us to buy, and feeling the reward of a growing savings jar. It is made for ages 5 and up, and parents often play along: the tricky levels fool plenty of grown-ups too.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you play Needs vs Wants?

Read each item's story, then tap "Need it" or "Want it". Every correctly spotted want drops its price into your savings jar. Sort 6 of 8 correctly to pass the level.

How many levels are there?

There are 100 levels, with 8 items in each level — 4 needs and 4 wants. The stories get trickier as you climb: the same item can be a need in one story and a want in another.

What is the difference between a need and a want?

A need keeps you safe, healthy, fed, or learning — like food, medicine, or a school book. A want is fun, tasty, or extra — like candy, toys, or a fifth teddy bear. Wants are not bad; they just come after needs and saving.

Who is Needs vs Wants for?

It is made for ages 5 and up — kids learning about money, and families who want a gentle first money lesson.

What does Needs vs Wants teach?

It teaches the order of smart spending: needs first, then saving a little, then wants with what is left — and that the story around an item decides which one it is.

Minesweeper looks like a guessing game. It is not. It is a thinking game wearing a guessing game's clothes. Every number on the board is a true clue, and with the right logic almost every board can be solved without a single wild guess. That is why this simple grid has stayed one of the world's most-played puzzles for decades — and why it teaches a lesson most games never touch: think before you click.

💣 How to play Minesweeper

1. Click any square to start. Your first click is always safe — the mines are only placed after it, so open the game with confidence.

2. Read the numbers. A revealed number tells you how many mines are hiding in the 8 squares touching it. No number at all? The whole safe area around it opens in one satisfying flood.

3. Flag the mines you have proved. Sure a square hides a mine? Right-click it on a computer, or long-press on a phone — or turn on Flag mode and flag with plain taps. Flags mark the danger and protect the square from an accidental click.

4. Clear every safe square to win. Reveal all the safe squares and you win — the board even flags the remaining mines for you. Touch one mine and it is boom, game over, try again.

🔢 What the numbers really mean

The numbers are the whole game, so read them properly. A 1 means exactly one of its touching squares is a mine. A 3 means three of them are. Two rules make you dangerous fast:

Corners and edges are gifts. A number in the corner has only 3 touching squares, and one on the edge has 5 — fewer suspects means easier logic. Always start your detective work at the edges of the opened area.

Numbers that share squares talk to each other. If a 1 and a 2 sit side by side touching the same unknown squares, the extra mine the 2 is counting must be in a square only the 2 can see. Comparing neighbours like this is how experts clear boards without guessing.

🏆 9 tips that actually win games

1. Open, then think. Make your safe first click, look at the whole opened area, and only then start deducing. Most losses happen in the first careless minute.

2. Flag as you prove. The moment a number proves a mine, flag it. Flags are your memory on the board — unflagged mines get clicked by tired eyes.

3. Satisfied numbers are free squares. When a 1 already touches a flag, every other square around it is safe. Clearing satisfied numbers is the fastest way to grow the board.

4. Learn the 1-2-1 pattern. When 1-2-1 sit in a row along a wall of unopened squares, the mines are under the two squares below the 1s, and the square below the 2 is safe. This one pattern solves a surprising share of boards.

5. Watch the counter. Mines minus flags tells you how many mines are still hiding. Late in the game it turns hard guesses into simple counting.

6. Work the edges first. Corners have 3 suspects, edges have 5, the middle has 8. Solve where the logic is cheapest.

7. When two areas are open, finish the easy one. A solved corner gives new numbers that often crack the hard area for free.

8. Guess only when forced — and guess smart. Sometimes two squares are a true 50/50. Take it early rather than late, and pick the square that opens the most new ground if it is safe.

9. Slow is smooth, smooth is fast. Your best time will come from a board you never had to restart. Careful beats fast, every single time.
